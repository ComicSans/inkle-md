# On the Sandbar {#ankunft}

{!The boat turns away almost before your kitbag is in the sand. "Tomorrow

The gale is full on. Spray goes horizontal across the island, and you walk
bent double, or you do not walk at all. Whatever has no hold out here belongs
to the sea now: there are only routes left, no places.

The cloud bank has half the sky, the sea runs in long ranks against the bar.
The wind leans against you when you stand still.

The light is going, and the cloud bank has grown. The wind has veered and
plucks at your jacket.

The evening is still, the sea lies flat around the bar. To the west stands
the cloud bank, still far off, still low. Whoever works here works against
it.

# Outside the Door {#vortreten}

The evening still stands outside the door; it has time. You step out onto

# The Way to the Cellar {#zum_keller}

The weather door stands open a finger's width and will not move, however
hard you brace against it. Behind it the water stands and holds against you.
Whatever still lies down there lies with the sea now. Only the tower
remains.

Twenty paces over to the engine house, through the narrow weather door on
the lee side, the short stair down into the cellar. {?: Light burns below;
the sea cable reaches into the cellar.}

The great gate on the seaward side the sea jammed shut long ago, so it is
the twenty paces on the lee side, the spray at your back. The weather door
gives, and you fall into the engine house more than you enter it. {?: The
glow of the oil lantern reaches just far enough for your own feet, and that

* Fill the lantern at the oil drum

The tap sits low on the drum. You fill the tank of the oil lantern, trim the
wick, one match: a small, portable circle of light, and the night in the
house has its opponent.

* Put wrench, sealing tape and can up on the plinth

You gather up what the generator will need before it can turn into a search:
the wrench, the sealing tape, the full petrol can. All of it up onto the
plinth, a hand's breadth higher than any puddle that ever stood here.
Whoever leaves the can standing below will be looking for it later in the
dark, under water.

* Wind the bulkhead doors shut on the seaward side

You go through the short passage, brace yourself against the wheel of the
outer door and wind it shut, then the inner one. Tight they never were - but
the sea now has to work its way through two bad seals instead of an open
door. That will not stop it. It buys you time.

* Pocket the wrench and the sealing tape

Both are small, the wrench surprisingly heavy. Both your hands are now taken
- none left free for the oil lantern.

+ Seal the fuel line, tighten the union

The fuel line is worn down to the thread, the union at the carburettor sits
loose. The old tape off, the new wound three times round and tight, rubbed
down with the thumb; then the wrench, half a turn, until it holds. This is
work for eyes: the tape has to lie where it should lie. {?: You work with

long.}

The can is heavy, the filler neck narrow; the petrol takes the time it needs
and wants to be watched where it goes. Then the tank is full to the cap.

You throw the decompression lever, take hold of the crank and swing it

# The Descent {#abstieg}

Rung by rung, with light and free hands. At the bottom you resolve not to do
that again in wind.

{?: At half height a gust takes the weight off your feet. You hold on until

Twice everything hangs on one hand: once when the gust comes, and once when

# Morning {#morgen}

The storm runs hour after hour against the tower and finds it occupied:
below, the generator holds its rhythm, up top the beam runs its rounds, and
you walk up and down between them, oil can in one hand, cleaning rag in the
other. At some point what comes through the panes is no longer its light,
but day.

In the grey light you shut down the generator and put the lever back. The
bar has grown smaller; the tower has not.

With the first bright water the boat comes around the bar. From the tiller a
nod, no more. No more was arranged.

# Grey Morning {#grauermorgen}

The night passes anyway. The storm blows itself out at some point, as every
storm does, and the first light shows what it has left behind: on the north
edge of the bar a fishing boat lies on its side, bow in the sand. Nothing
moves on deck.

Your lamp was dark when it was needed. {?: In the cellar of the engine house

The boat picks you up in the morning, as arranged. It will be a quiet
crossing.

# Under the Pines {#erschlagen}

It is not with a crash that the wood comes, more of a sigh, and then it
takes the sky away from you. The sand is soft on the north edge. It changes

# The Water in the Cellar {#ertrunken}

The sea does not come into the cellar as a wave. It comes as a surge: first
around your ankles, then above your knees, and up the stair it presses
against you, pulls you down and swallows you.

Your adventure ends here.
