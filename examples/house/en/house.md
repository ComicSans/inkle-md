The lock turns twice. You feel better at once. Right after that comes the

Three thousand books, of which two thousand nine hundred are fakes: painted

# The Door with the Towel {#bathroom}

A bathroom. A tub on lion's feet, half full, the water still warm. Nobody
there. The soap has been used.

* Hang the towel back and leave quietly
* Look into the mirror cabinet

In the mirror the room stands as it is. The crucifix in your pocket grows
unpleasantly warm, which for now you ignore.

In the mirror the room stands as it is, except for the tub. In it sits
a figure, regarding you amiably. Then, very slowly, it lifts a hand out of
the water and waves. You spin round: the tub behind you is empty. In the
mirror it is still waving. Your FEAR rises.

You close the door behind you with more care than doors usually deserve.

# The Tower Stairs {#tower-stairs}

A spiral staircase so narrow you must carry the lantern ahead of you like an
apology. At the top, a round room: a chair with straps on the armrests, a
window, moonlight.

# The Tower Room {#tower}

You wake, and the first thing you know is: this is not your bed. The second:
you are sitting. Your mouth tastes of wine and cotton wool, your heart is
hammering all the way into your fingertips, and under your hands lie the
armrests of a chair fitted with straps.

They have not been fastened. That seems less like mercy than like routine:
where would you go. Your FEAR rises.

# The White Lady {#lady}

{visits(lady) == 1: In the moonlight stands a woman who casts no shadow and looks as if she had been waiting for years, for centuries, for a visitor.|She is still standing in the moonlight, waiting to see whether anything worth hearing will come from you.}

{visits(lady) == 1: "New here?" she asks. Her voice sounds like one that has not been used in a long time and declines to complain about it.}

* Stay polite and listen
* Ask the way out
* Ask what happened to her
* Take your leave

She looks at you for a long time. "Good manners," she says. "Those have
grown rare here." She was stranded here years ago, also in the rain. "The
master," she says, "has a name he keeps out of the books. It is written in
the diary in his study, and on the archway, under the ivy. Speak it when he
stands before you, and then see to it that you get away quickly." Then she
leans forward and tells it to you, slowly and twice, the way one entrusts a
child with something important.

She points at the stairs. "The only door," she says, and loses interest in
you for a moment, at a speed that would be insulting if you did not currently
have other worries.

She looks down at herself as if she had to check. "I was impolite," she says
at last. No more than that, and you do not ask, because her gaze goes to the
chair with the straps as she says it.

When you turn round once more at the door, she is standing at the window,
looking out like someone who knows the view by heart and checks it anyway.

# The Stairs Down {#tower-out}

{knows("BETAEUBT"): At the bottom the spiral stairs end at the narrow door. The bolt is set from the other side. The door is old. The frame is older.|At the bottom the narrow door stands open, the way you left it.}

{choice_count() == 1: There is only one way, and it leads down.}

* Lift the door off its hinges with your pocketknife
* Use your shoulder
* Climb out of the window and down the ivy
* Climb down

Bolts are a matter of opinion; hinges are physics. The door leans into your
arms like a tired colleague.

The bolt holds. The frame does not.

The door wins the first two attempts.

On the third it gives way; the house does not win every time.

The ivy carries you one storey down, where a window stands open. You climb
in and are standing in the library, as if this were a customary way of
entering libraries.

The ivy is past its best years. It tears, you fall the last stretch and in
through the open window below. The library receives you with a carpet that
has seen worse.
